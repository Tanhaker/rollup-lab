Proof of work / SHA-256

Break a chain on purpose

Every block hashes its own data together with the hash of the block before it. Mine the whole chain, then go back and change the data in Block 1. Every block after it turns red instantly — you didn't touch them, but their inputs changed. That cascade is what people mean by immutability.

Chain status Nothing mined yet — every block is invalid until it meets the difficulty target.

What you just did

Mining means brute-forcing the nonce until the block's SHA-256 hash starts with the required number of zeros. There is no shortcut — you try, you check, you try again. Raising the difficulty by one zero multiplies the expected work by 16, which is why the counter jumps so hard between settings. Rewriting history means redoing that work for the tampered block and every block after it, faster than the rest of the network extends the honest chain. On a real network that is economically hopeless, and that is the entire security argument.

What each block commits to

Every block hashes the exact string index|data|nonce|prevHash. Block 1's prevHash is 64 zeros — a genesis placeholder — and every other block takes the hash of the block above it, which is why the prev-hash field is filled in for you rather than typed. Change a single character anywhere in that string and SHA-256 produces a completely unrelated 64-character output, so the block no longer meets its target and neither does anything downstream of it.
